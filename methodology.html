Methodology

How ARGUS stays honest about an industry built on secrecy.

Claim-strength labels

Every customer link and incident in ARGUS carries a mandatory claim-strength label. Allegations are never stated as fact:

Confirmed
Vendor-stated, court-admitted, or government-acknowledged.
Forensically attributed
Technical attribution by Citizen Lab, Amnesty Security Lab, Google Threat Intelligence Group, or equivalent forensic work.
Reported
Reputable investigative reporting (named outlet). The claim exists in print; it is not independently verified by ARGUS.
Alleged
The claim exists but sourcing is thin, disputed, or denied.
Denied
The vendor or government explicitly denies the claim. Denials are recorded as first-class rows with their own sources — both sides display.

Labeling rules

  1. Every factual cell has a source. No source, no row. The provenance spine is not optional.
  2. Copy rule: "Citizen Lab reported that…", "the Predator Files allege…" — never "Vendor X sold to country Y" unless the claim is confirmed.
  3. Dockets over headlines. Legal outcomes are quoted from primary records. Example: the WhatsApp v. NSO entry reflects the final judgment (November 12, 2025: $4,447,190 plus a permanent injunction), not the earlier jury figure.
  4. No victim PII beyond published reporting. Victim descriptions quote the source's own characterization; ARGUS does no independent identification work.
  5. No live operational data. No C2 domains, no infection IOCs, no exploit details beyond what the cited public report contains.
  6. Corrections are versioned. Records carry update dates; material corrections get changelog entries. Non-confirmed claims are re-checked, not silently kept.

Hard boundaries (non-goals)

ARGUS documents the industry; it never ships, links to, or explains how to obtain or operate spyware. No non-public data. No leaked databases mirrored in full. No real-time surveillance feed. No legal conclusions — "sued", "listed", "sanctioned" are recorded as events with citations.

Data collection never bypasses access controls: no CAPTCHA, login, or paywall circumvention.

Data pipeline

The US Consolidated Screening List is pulled mechanically (free, no key, updated daily) and matched against vendor names and aliases with word-boundary matching; false positives are reviewed and excluded. Curated tables (vendors, products, corporate entities, legal actions, sources) are human-verified against the cited sources. Incident and customer-link curation passes 1 and 2 are live: every row carries a named source and a claim-strength label, per the rules above.
